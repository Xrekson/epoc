Five 15v15 map blueprints

At 30 players a map needs more routes, more cover density and more spawn buffer than a 6v6 map. Every layout below offers at least three lanes, a loud central fight and quiet flanks, so no squad gets trapped. Each plan is drawn to scale, with a meter grid and a scale bar.

Foundry

Domination, 3 flags, mirrored

Setting
A steel mill. Cranes, ore hoppers and a roofed casting hall in the middle.
Flow
North and south lanes are long, open-cover yards with flag A and C. The center hall holds B and has four doors plus a catwalk, so it is the meat grinder.
Power spots
Crane cabs overlook both outer flags. They are reached by one ladder each, which gives snipers a risk.
Why 15v15
Three flags let each team split 5/5/5 or stack one lane. Flanking tunnels under the hall stop a stack from being a lock.

Dockside Cutoff

Attack and defend, 2 sequential objectives

Setting
A night container port. Attackers land from boats, defenders hold the warehouse vault.
Flow
Objective 1 is the pier crane control, close to the landing so the first push is fast. Objective 2 is the vault, reached through two flank roads or a narrow center alley.
Power spots
Stacked containers form defender high ground. Attackers can open shortcuts by blowing a container door.
Why 15v15
Two flanks and a center alley force the defense to split. A fallback to the vault gives them a second chance after losing the pier.

Highrise Atlas

Hardpoint, rotating zone, diagonal spawns

Setting
A financial district with nine towers, street canyons and skybridges.
Flow
The hardpoint rotates through five intersections, always ending near the center tower. Teams spawn on opposite corners, so every rotation is a fresh fight.
Power spots
Skybridges give a fast cross-map route and a clear target. The center tower roof covers point 3 but is exposed on all sides.
Why 15v15
The zone is wide enough to hold with 10 players while 5 flank through the towers. Interior stairwells keep vertical fights from becoming a dead end.

Salt Flats Outpost

Capture the flag with vehicles

Setting
A desert salt flat with two outposts, a central mesa and canyon walls on the north and south edges.
Flow
Open ground in the middle favors buggies and long-range rifles. Narrow canyon routes along the edges favor close combat and flag runners.
Power spots
The mesa has two ramps and a cave under it. Holding the top covers both lanes but cuts you off from your base.
Why 15v15
It is the biggest map of the set, so each team can field 4 vehicles and still have 9 or more on foot. Spread-out cover prevents the flag from camping.

Monastery Ridge

Attack and defend, uphill, asymmetric

Setting
A mountain monastery. A switchback road climbs from the valley to a bell tower.
Flow
Attackers take the gatehouse first, then the bell tower. The road is the safe, slow route. A goat trail along the cliff is fast but exposed, so it is a high-risk flank.
Power spots
Defenders hold terraces above every switchback. Attackers get smoke cover from the roadside ruins.
Why 15v15
Terraces give defenders layered fallback positions. Fifteen attackers can pressure the road and the cliff trail at the same time.

Shared design rules

  • Respawn to first contact: 20 to 30 seconds on foot.
  • No lane wider than one sightline for a sniper, and no lane without at least two cover pieces per 15 meters.
  • Every objective has three approaches and one blind-side entrance.
  • Spawn zones are shielded from the objective by a building or terrain so nobody gets spawn-trapped.
